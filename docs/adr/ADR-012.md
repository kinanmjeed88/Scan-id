# ADR-012 — Candidate-quality gates before any derived asset exists; derived provenance recorded on the asset

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-09

## Context

Two defects were reported against Smart Recognition and both were reproduced
against the code, not against a screenshot.

**(a) Background fragments became ordinary documents.** `segmentDecoded`
filtered merged foreground components only by `area >= total * .015`,
`width >= 10`, `height >= 10`, `fill >= .4` and `width * height <= total * .95`.
`fill >= .4` is satisfied trivially by any *solid* region, which is exactly what
a shadow strip, a table edge or a vignette band is. There was no aspect bound,
no frame-contact test, no usable-crop minimum and no candidate cap. Every
surviving region then became, unconditionally, a derived file
(`assets.importImage`), a `DocumentRecord` and a `DocumentItem` in
`SmartIntake._applyMultiDocument`; regions whose outline could not be resolved
got an axis-aligned `cropRegion` with `trustworthy: false`, which routes to
`autoLayoutStatus() == sizeUnconfirmed` and renders in `OffSheetTray`. So the
extra editor items were real documents with real crop files, not a rendering
fault. `SegmentCandidate.reason` was written but read by nobody, so the one
diagnostic that existed (`region-too-small`) was discarded and the region was
still turned into a document.

**(b) Derived assets could be committed without anything explaining them.**
`_sourceAssetIds` classified an asset as derived *solely* by scanning
`documents[].sides[].processedAsset` and path-matching `workingPath` /
`thumbnailPath`; any asset no record named was treated as an authoritative
SOURCE. Meanwhile `_applyMultiDocument` and `_refreshDocument` wrote each
derived file **and saved the asset list per region**, while records and items
were merged only at the end of `run()` / `reprocess()`. A failure, a
`RevisionConflict` or a `cancellation.throwIfCancelled()` between those two
points left persisted derived crops with no record. `Project` validation
(`items.every((i) => assetIds.contains(i.assetId))`) then made the final
`copyWith` throw, the batch aborted, and the database kept the record-less
crops — which the next reprocess re-analysed as photographs, appending a second
document for the same physical card.

## Decision

**1. Candidate quality is decided in the segmenter, before any crop exists.**
`segmentDecoded` measures each merged region once (`_measure`) and refuses it
with a typed `RegionRejection` when a measurement that cannot hold for a
plausible document does hold:

| Rejection | Measurement | Bound |
|---|---|---|
| `frameArtifact` | frame edges touched | `>= 2` |
| `implausibleAspect` | long / short side | `> 8.0` |
| `unusableCrop` | short side of the crop at preview resolution | `< 48 px` |
| `candidateCap` | accepted regions per photo | `> 12` (largest kept) |

Refused regions are returned in `SegmentationResult.rejected` with the
measurements that decided them — never in `candidates` — and are surfaced as ONE
aggregated Arabic diagnostic per photo (`rejectedRegionsMessage`) plus a count
on `AutomaticLayoutReport.rejectedRegions`, counted separately from
`notDetected` because a refused region created nothing.

**2. The bounds come from the catalog and from measurement, not from a
screenshot.** The most extreme proportion the application itself sells is the
ration card at 287 / 52 = 5.52, so a bound below that would refuse a catalogued
document. Measured over the fixture set this repository ships (`test/imaging`,
`test/application`), run through a port of the segmenter's own arithmetic — the
same Otsu binning, the same 360 px working frame with `Interpolation.average`,
the same 2 px border median, the same 8 % region margin and the same `.round()`
semantics — with `img.fillRect`'s inclusive corners and `copyResize`'s kernels
taken from the pinned `image` 4.x source rather than assumed:

- genuine components: aspect **1.26 – 5.49**, frame sides **0 – 1**, crop short
  side **215 – 670 px**. The minimum is the distant document and the
  ration-card proportion, whose 52 mm side is short by nature; 1.26 is a card
  rotated 18° in the photo. An artificial 14-region grid used only to exercise
  the candidate cap reaches 139 px and is still 2.9× the floor.
- artifact components: aspect **12.00 – 22.50** for strips and slivers, frame
  sides **2 – 3** for vignettes and cut-off regions, crop short side
  **43 – 104 px**.

8.0 therefore sits 1.46× above the most extreme genuine document and 33 % below
the narrowest strip. 48 px sits 4.5× below the smallest genuine document crop;
it is a floor, not a discriminator, and its real effect is that a region already
measured as too small to crop is now *refused with a reason* instead of being
emitted as a `region-too-small` candidate that became a document. It can only
bind for small sources: once the 8 % margin is added, a region that clears the
segmenter's own 10 px box floor crops to at least `previewLongEdge / 20` px, so
with the editor's 1200 px preview the branch cannot fire at all.

**3. No ink, colour or texture gate.** A content-uniformity discriminator was
evaluated and rejected. Structurally it cannot work: the fraction of a crop that
differs from the crop's own median — the measure that would separate a printed
card from a blank one — scores **0.000** for BOTH a genuine low-contrast card
and a solid dark strip on the shipped fixtures, because both are uniform, so no
separating threshold exists between them. On real documents it is worse, not
better: a faded, washed-out, low-resolution or blank-margin genuine card is
low-structure too. Any ink threshold that refuses the artifact also refuses such
a document, which is why the shipped gates measure only geometry and frame
contact.

**4. Ambiguity is preserved, not refused.** A blank sheet, a printed page that
is not an identity document, a soft shadow band and a solid dark rectangle are
geometrically plausible and are *not* invalid; they stay candidates, land
off-sheet with `sizeConfirmed: false` and enter the review queue. Refusing them
would be a content judgement the measurements above show cannot be made safely.

**5. Origin is recorded on the asset, never inferred.** `ImageAsset` gains a
nullable `derivedFrom`, written by every path that creates a derived crop
(`asDerivedOf`) and preserved by every path that rewrites one
(`LocalImageEditor.createRevision`, `ProjectService.replaceImage`). The field is
optional and a missing key means "no provenance recorded", never "not derived",
so the schema stays **v5** and existing projects and backups open unchanged.
`reconcileDerivedProvenance` backfills only relationships the project's own
records already prove, treats a recorded value as authoritative, deletes
nothing, and reports a `derivedFrom` naming a missing asset as a *broken chain*
— preserved and surfaced, never guessed away.

**6. One commit boundary per batch, staged per image.** Derived files are
written first; assets, records, items and groups are written in a single
`projects.save`. Each image accumulates into per-image lists that join the batch
only if that image completed, so a failure between two regions commits nothing
partial. Unreferenced crop files are the explicit, recoverable residue of a
partial run and are reported by the existing `StorageMaintenance.findOrphans`.

## Consequences

- A refused region costs nothing; an accepted one costs a derived file, a record
  and an editor item the user must delete by hand. Deciding early is therefore
  the cheap direction, and refusal never crops, overwrites or touches the
  original (ADR-003).
- `AutoLayoutEngine` remains the sole placement authority (ADR-002): the gates
  decide only whether a region is a *candidate*, never where it goes.
- Kind classification stays separate from candidate quality. A region can match
  a catalogued proportion exactly and still be refused a confirmed size, because
  `sizeConfirmed` requires a trustworthy boundary (`catalogSize` is null
  otherwise) and R4 requires the classification to clear its own floor.
- **A refusal is reported, never silently dropped.** The gates decide whether a
  region is a candidate; the user still has to be told what was thrown away and
  why, or a correct refusal looks exactly like a lost document. So the report
  carries the refusals as data — `AutomaticLayoutReport.rejectedByReason`, a
  tally built by the same `rejectionTally` the per-image warning uses, with an
  initializer assert that the tally accounts for `rejectedRegions` — and three
  surfaces word it for their space: `rejectedSummary` (count, measured
  categories, "nothing was added", the recovery promise) on both editor
  commands, `rejectedHeadline` on its own line of the project screen's intake
  summary, and one aggregated per-image warning. Only categories a gate actually
  decided are ever named; a reason with a zero count is absent, because naming
  it would be a diagnostic the pipeline never measured.
- **Known residual risk, accepted deliberately — not eliminated.** A crisp solid
  strip drawn to the ration card's own catalogued proportion and a genuine
  printed ration card were measured through the same harness on the same
  fixture construction:

  | basis | shadow band | genuine ration card |
  |---|---|---|
  | work-frame box aspect — what the 8.0 bound reads | 5.6000 | 5.6250 |
  | preview region aspect | 5.4785 | 5.4997 |
  | derived crop aspect (158 × 866 / 158 × 869) | 5.4810 | 5.5000 |
  | fill | 1.000 | 0.986 |
  | frame border sides | 0 | 0 |
  | area fraction | 0.0922 | 0.0913 |

  On every aspect basis the band measures *lower* than the real document and is
  accepted with it, so tightening a bound refuses the genuine ration card first.
  On fill it measures *higher*, so a uniformity rule is inverted too. The two
  are pipeline-indistinguishable; this is Case 4 — preserved, routed to review
  under `AutomationMode.reviewAll`, and never silently accepted. What bounds the
  risk is that review routing, not detection: `automationMode` is `reviewAll`
  and `reviewReasons` is non-empty for every recognised record, so no document
  reaches the sheet unreviewed. An earlier draft of this file quoted "5.62
  against 5.49" for this pair; those two numbers came from different bases
  (box aspect and region aspect) and are corrected by the table above. A strip
  with *soft* edges (the common shadow case) is already safe: without a
  trustworthy quad no catalog size can be claimed at all.
- **Frame contact is not a rejection rule, and touching documents are the cost.**
  Two narrow cards laid edge to edge with no visible desk between them form one
  connected component measuring aspect 11.25, which `implausibleAspect` refuses,
  so that photo takes the single-document path instead of yielding two cards.
  With a visible gap all three documents are kept. Refusing "every region that
  touches the frame" was rejected by constraint and would be worse: the refusal
  is at least reported with its category, the original stays whole, and the user
  can crop either card by hand from the library.
- `DetectionAnalysis.orientation` remains unconsumed. Its input was corrected
  (`cropHeldAspect` preserves orientation, where `_shapeSide` normalized it away
  and made the estimate constant per kind), but every branch is
  `confident: false`, so nothing may apply it without asking the user.

## Alternatives considered

- **An ink / internal-structure gate** — evaluated, rejected (see 3).
- **A tighter aspect bound (5.6 – 6.0)** — refuses the genuine ration card
  (measured 5.6000 / 5.6250 / 5.4785 / 5.4997 depending on basis, i.e. on every
  basis the band sits at or below the real card), or leaves it under a 1.10×
  margin. Tuning a bound to one artifact is overfitting; 8.0 is kept because it
  is justified by the catalogued extreme (287 / 52 = 5.52) and measured against
  two populations, not against one artifact.
- **A uniformity ("fill") gate — `fill >= .99` means artifact** — measured and
  rejected. It separates the crisp shadow band (fill 1.000) from the printed
  ration card (fill 0.986) by **1.4 %**, and it runs the wrong way for genuine
  documents: blank paper, a printed page and a washed-out or faded card all
  measure fill 1.000 on these fixtures. A rule that keeps a real card by 1.4 %
  and loses blank and faded cards is not a rule, it is a coin toss calibrated on
  one screenshot.
- **Routing narrow regions somewhere other than review** — there is no headroom
  to gain. `const automationMode` is `AutomationMode.reviewAll` and
  `reviewReasons(record)` is non-empty for every record carrying a recognition
  block unless the user resolved it, so a genuine ration card and the shadow band
  already land in the same queue for the same reason. The only available lever
  would be to suppress review for a region, which moves risk in the wrong
  direction.
- **Auto-rotating on the orientation estimate** — refused: every branch of
  `DetectionAnalysis.orientation` is `confident: false`, and the field has no
  consumer in `lib/`. Rotating a document on an untrusted estimate would be a
  redesign, not a fix.
- **Classifying a region invalid because its quad is untrustworthy** — refused:
  it would discard genuine documents whose outline is merely hard to find, which
  is the case the unresolved-region review path exists for.
- **A parallel asset-state system, review queue or transaction framework** —
  refused by constraint; Sembast plus the filesystem cannot be atomic, so the
  ordering is made explicit and the residue made recoverable instead.
- **Inferring origin from dimensions, filename or appearance** — refused by
  constraint and unsound: a crop of a card and a photograph of a card are
  indistinguishable that way.
- **Bumping the schema to make `derivedFrom` mandatory** — unnecessary; an
  optional field is backward and forward compatible and keeps v5 projects,
  backups and exports byte-compatible.

## Evidence and its limits

The numbers above come from three different kinds of evidence, and they are not
interchangeable.

| Kind | What it proves | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Port of the segmenter's arithmetic (Python, not shipped) | Which regions a given pixel fixture produces, and the measurements each gate reads — used to choose bounds and to fix every asserted number *before* it was written | outside the repository |
| Production Dart tests | That the real segmenter, corner detector, classifier, repositories and layout engine together produce the project the user ends up with | `test/imaging/document_segmenter_gate_test.dart`, `test/application/false_positive_document_test.dart`, `test/application/document_classification_path_test.dart`, `test/application/derived_provenance_test.dart`, `test/application/ration_proportion_test.dart`, `test/presentation/unresolved_candidate_badge_test.dart` |
| Production Dart tests over the real file-backed stack, driven at controller level | That the refusal tally reaches the message the user actually reads, through `ProjectService`, `LocalAssetRepository`, `LocalImageEditor` and `LayoutEditorController` — real temp directories and real PNG decoding, no stubbed repositories | `test/application/rejection_feedback_test.dart`, `test/presentation/rejection_feedback_ui_test.dart`, `test/application/provenance_rewrite_paths_test.dart` |
| CI | That the shipped tree formats, analyses, passes the whole suite, passes the independent export audit and builds for Android and Windows | `.github/workflows/verify.yml` |

The port is evidence about *measurement*, never about shipped behaviour: it
reproduces this file's arithmetic and the pinned `image` package's resize and
fill kernels, but only the Dart tests and CI speak for the application. Every
fixture geometry asserted in a Dart test was measured through the port first,
and the port was itself checked against fixtures whose outcome was already known.

`test/application/ration_proportion_test.dart` pins the residual risk rather
than the thresholds. It asserts that the band and the card produce the *same
decision* — same document and item counts, same kind, same size confirmation,
same review state — rendered as text and compared for equality, so the test
fails the moment anything in the pipeline starts telling them apart. That is a
deliberate inversion: a test asserting "the artifact is refused" would have to
be written by tuning a threshold until it passed, which is the overfitting this
ADR forbids. The test also asserts the direction of the measurement
(`band < card`, gap < 0.05) so that nobody re-derives a bound between them, and
carries a positive control — the same three documents with a visible desk gap —
to show the touching-pair refusal is about touching and not about the
ration-card proportion.

Three limits are stated plainly rather than left implicit:

- **No screenshots were ever attached to the report.** The false-positive
  fixtures are synthetic reconstructions of the described symptoms — several
  documents plus a frame band, a strip, a sliver and a blank sheet — not the
  reporter's photographs. They reproduce the *defect class* through the real
  pipeline; they are not evidence about one particular photograph.
- **No physical-device testing was done**, and no OCR engine ships
  (`UnavailableOcrEngine`), so nothing here claims on-device behaviour, Arabic
  document accuracy, or any understanding of a document's text or identity
  numbers.
- **The residual risk above is measured on synthetic fixtures only.** The band
  and the card were drawn at the ration card's catalogued proportion on a
  uniform desk; no real photograph of an Iraqi ration card, no real shadow and
  no physical device took part. The honest claim is therefore *pipeline
  indistinguishability on these fixtures, bounded by mandatory review and by
  immutable originals* — not that the class is eliminated, and not that any
  accuracy on real documents has been established. Closing it would need a
  signal this build does not have (content understanding), and no OCR engine
  ships.
